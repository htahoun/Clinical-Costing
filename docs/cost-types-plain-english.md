# Cost Types — Plain English Guide

**Vision Dental Clinic L.L.C · Licence MF4159**
Abu Dhabi Clinical Cost Data Collection (ADCCDC)

---

## What this page is for

DOH makes us sort every dirham we spend into a fixed set of boxes. They call these
**Cost Types**. There are **15 boxes** and every expense we have must land in exactly one
of them.

This page explains each box in ordinary language, so anyone on the team — Accounts,
Reception, IT — can look at an invoice and know where it goes without needing to read
the regulation.

**The golden rule:** one expense, one box. Never two.

---

## The people we pay (boxes 1–4)

These four boxes cover every salary in the clinic. The only question is *what kind of
job* the person does.

### 1. `SWNurs` — Nurse Salaries
**Who:** Registered nurses.

Only people who are actually licensed nurses. If someone is called a "nurse" informally
but is really a chair-side helper, they belong in box 3 or 4 instead.

### 2. `SWDoc` — Doctors' Salaries
**Who:** Our dentists — full-time and part-time.

This includes **commissions**, not just basic salary. If a dentist earns a percentage of
what they bill, that percentage goes here too.

> *Note:* the DOH template labels this "Department #1 — e.g. Dental". That's because a
> hospital would have many doctor departments. We only have one: Dental.

### 3. `SWAllied` — Allied Staff Salaries
**Who:** The trained clinical support people who are not nurses and not dentists.

- Dental assistants
- Dental technicians
- Radiology assistants
- CSSD technician (the person who sterilises instruments)

Simple way to think about it: **they touch clinical work, but they're not the dentist
and not a nurse.**

### 4. `SWNonClin` — Other Salaries
**Who:** Everybody who keeps the business running but never treats a patient.

- Reception, Cashier, Insurance Coordinator
- Clinic Manager, PRO, IT Administrator
- Finance Manager, Marketing Executive
- Cleaner, Housekeeping staff, Driver

Simple way to think about it: **if the clinic were a shop instead of a clinic, these
jobs would still exist.**

## The things we use on patients (boxes 5–10)

### 5. `Laboratory` — Lab Work
Dental lab work is the big one for us — crowns, bridges, dentures, aligners, whether
made in-house or sent out.

The DOH list also names haematology, biochemistry and microbiology labs. Those are
hospital services; for us this box is mostly **dental lab**.

### 6. `Imaging` — Pictures of the Patient
X-rays and anything else that photographs inside the patient. The DOH list names CT,
ultrasound, MRI and mammography — for us it's **X-ray, OPG and CBCT**, plus the cost of
keeping those machines serviced.

### 7. `Pharmacy` — Medicines
Medicines we buy and give or dispense to patients, plus pharmacy supplies.

### 8. `Prostheses` — Things We Fit Into or Onto the Patient
The parts that stay with the patient permanently or long-term:

- Dental implant screws
- Abutments
- Crown caps
- Braces
- Retainers

Simple way to think about it: **the patient walks out with it inside their mouth.**

### 9. `MS` — Medical & Surgical Supplies
The everyday consumables we use up and throw away:

Gloves, masks, gowns, caps, shoe covers, syringes, needles, drapes, gauze, cotton,
tapes, blades, sutures, stitches, antiseptics, IV sets, oxygen masks, test strips.

Simple way to think about it: **single-use, clinical, and it goes in the bin at the end
of the appointment.**

> **Careful — the difference between box 8 and box 9:**
> A crown stays with the patient → box 8 (`Prostheses`).
> The glove the dentist wore while fitting it → box 9 (`MS`).

### 10. `Hotel` — Feeding and Transporting Patients
**For our clinic this box means: patient nutrition — juice, food and refreshments — and
the car transport we provide for patients.**

DOH's own wording for this box mentions patient meals, bedsheets, gowns and TV, because
the rule was written with hospitals in mind. We have no beds, so the bedding half of that
description does not apply to us. The **food and patient-transport half does**, and that
is what we post here.

> **Why this matters:** a DOH reviewer looking at a clinic with no beds will ask why
> there is a `Hotel` figure at all. The answer above is our written explanation — keep it
> with the audit file so we are not improvising the answer later.

## Running the business (boxes 11–14)

### 11. `GS` — Goods & Services
The catch-all for everything the business buys that isn't clinical and isn't a salary:

Paper, printer, stationery, uniforms, furniture, IT hardware, maintenance & repairs,
pest control, Tasneef audits, consulting services, software subscriptions, newspaper,
courier, petrol, vehicle renewal, vehicle insurance, vehicle maintenance.

Simple way to think about it: **office and admin running costs.**

### 12. `Depreciation` — Wear and Tear
Not money leaving the bank this month. This is the accounting way of saying *"our
equipment lost some value this year."*

Applies to: medical equipment, IT equipment, furniture, vehicles.

Simple way to think about it: **we bought the dental chair once, but it wears out a
little every year — that yearly slice is what goes here.**

### 13. `OHF` — Facility Overheads
The cost of having a building at all:

Electricity, water, internet, building rent, employee insurance renewal, malpractice
insurance renewal.

Simple way to think about it: **we'd pay these even on a day with zero patients.**

### 14. `OHC` — Corporate / Head Office Overheads
Costs that come from above the clinic level, or that promote the business:

Marketing campaigns, website, advertising, training.

---

## The one that doesn't apply to us

### 15. `NABMs` — Negative Adjustment for Budgeted Mandate Services
Government-funded insurance adjustments.

**Not applicable to us.** This box stays empty / zero.

## Quick reference table

| # | DOH code | Plain name | One-line test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `SWNurs` | Nurse salaries | Are they a registered nurse? |
| 2 | `SWDoc` | Dentist salaries | Are they a dentist? (include commission) |
| 3 | `SWAllied` | Clinical support salaries | Clinical, but not dentist or nurse |
| 4 | `SWNonClin` | Admin & support salaries | Never treats a patient |
| 5 | `Laboratory` | Lab work | Made in a lab (mostly dental lab) |
| 6 | `Imaging` | X-ray & scans | A picture of the patient |
| 7 | `Pharmacy` | Medicines | A drug |
| 8 | `Prostheses` | Fitted items | Patient keeps it in their mouth |
| 9 | `MS` | Clinical consumables | Single-use, then binned |
| 10 | `Hotel` | Patient food & transport | Feeding a patient, or driving them |
| 11 | `GS` | Goods & services | Office / admin running costs |
| 12 | `Depreciation` | Wear and tear | Equipment losing value, not cash out |
| 13 | `OHF` | Facility overheads | Paid even with zero patients |
| 14 | `OHC` | Head office & marketing | Promoting or supporting the business |
| 15 | `NABMs` | Government mandate adjustment | Not applicable — leave at zero |

## Two traps to watch when we start posting

These are places where the same item honestly appears on two DOH lists, so we need one
house rule and everyone must follow it.

**Trap 1 — Furniture and IT hardware appear in both box 11 (`GS`) and box 12
(`Depreciation`).**
House rule to agree: if we **expense** it in the year we buy it → `GS`. If we
**capitalise** it and write it down over several years → `Depreciation`, and only the
yearly slice goes in. Never both, or we count the same money twice.

**Trap 2 — Vehicles appear in both boxes too.**
That one is fine and correct: petrol, insurance and maintenance are *running* costs
(`GS`); the van losing value is *wear and tear* (`Depreciation`). Two different things
about the same vehicle.

## Housekeeping notes

- This list is **15 boxes**. The DOH Standard's Appendix 1 defines 14; `NABMs` is the
  15th and is not applicable to us.
- `Pharmacy` (7) did not appear in the percentage split provided separately. To be
  confirmed when the final figures arrive.
- `Hotel` (10) is **not** near zero for us, despite what the hospital-oriented wording
  suggests — see the box above. It carries real patient food and transport cost, and it
  appears on surgical and implant treatment codes.
- Nothing on this page is a calculation. It is a filing guide only.
